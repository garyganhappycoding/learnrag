# Who You Are, Gary — An Extended Profile

Across many conversations, projects, and half-finished ideas, a very specific person has come into focus. This isn't a generic personality sketch — it's built from what you've actually said, built, and decided. Here's the fuller picture.

## The Foundations

You're 19, based in Malaysia, and academically you've operated at the top of the curve for a long time. You finished your Matriculation at Kolej Matrikulasi Johor with a perfect 4.00 CGPA. During that time you weren't just a top student — you were President of the PAL (Peer Assisted Learning) High Council and a Student Representative (JPP), and before that, in secondary school, you held a string of leadership roles: president of your school-level and district-level Student Leaders Board in Batu Pahat, various club chairperson positions, a seat on the Johor Student Leaders Council, and a spot on the Johor Sin Chew Daily Student Journalist Team. You've also collected concrete competitive wins — a Gold Medal at the PAL Symposium, 1st Runner-Up and Best Slide Deck at the CyberSAFE Challenge Trophy 2024, and 3rd Place at the Johor Youth English Debate Competition 2024.

That track record isn't incidental to who you are now — it's the evidence base you draw on. When you were building your application for President of the Psychology Student Union (PSU) at HELP University, this leadership history was the core of your pitch, alongside your stated motivation: bridging psychology students with real industry exposure through practitioner visits, job shadowing, internship vlog competitions, and volunteering opportunities. You care about the PSU role specifically because it opens a relationship with Dr Victor, a professor with strong industry connections who's willing to write custom recommendation letters for members — and you want that letter to support a 2+1 program with a year of overseas study, possibly in Australia.

You're trilingual — Mandarin as your native language, English at a strong working level, and Bahasa Malaysia — and that isn't a side skill; it runs underneath almost everything: your studygram content, your property marketing, your course materials, even your Kantian ethics essays.

## The Builder Who Learned to Code Through Doing

You have no formal coding background. Every one of your projects has been built through iterative guidance, editing files manually in VS Code on Windows, and deploying through GitHub to Vercel. That's an important detail — it means the volume and range of what you've shipped isn't the product of formal training, but of persistence and a very high tolerance for figuring things out as you go.

The list is genuinely long:

- **My Life OS** — a personal life-management Flutter app tracking life fields, earning-power projects, a merit/demerit log, reflections, and AI-generated coach reports. It's live on Google Play. You've iterated on it repeatedly: adding a history view for merits, weekly/monthly AI report generation, persistent completed-task strikethroughs, an archive/unarchive flow, a hexagon radar chart, and a coach-mark onboarding walkthrough.
- **Photo Organizer** — a Flutter Android app for grouping device photos and videos and syncing them into real Android albums, useful for things like Instagram's photo picker. You built many-to-many photo-group membership, video support, bulk move-not-copy syncing, group management, and a "Memory / On This Day" screen — and you tracked down and fixed a real asset-cache bug along the way.
- **XPLog (累经簿)** — a gamified life-logging web app built with Next.js, Firebase, and d3: XP and leveling mechanics, a merit/demerit log, ROI tracking, a Daily Insights Zettelkasten module, and a force-directed graph view. You've since added a radar chart, per-skill rename/delete with cascading XP recalculation, and per-entry editing.
- **Worthy** — your hackathon entry for the Talentbank Career OS Challenge: a "living portfolio" platform for Malaysian fresh graduates where AI extracts structured portfolio entries from social-media-style posts, with employer matching and mentor connections built in.
- **OneStock** — a spin-wheel-based public-speaking and communication practice platform. The idea started from watching spinwheel-style practice videos online; you scoped a full loop where a user spins to land on an S&P 500 stock, gets 15 minutes to research it, then speaks about it for a self-chosen duration, with a persistent collection system that only reveals financial data after they've actually spoken. You built the wheel engine to be subject-agnostic from day one, with plans to expand into SPM/IGCSE physics, biology, and chemistry decks.
- **A personal finance dashboard** — reading from a Notion database fed by a Telegram-to-Make.com pipeline, splitting your income across a 50/30/20 structure (daily expenses, investment via Moomoo, and a "Dream" account for entertainment and travel goals), with plans for an interactive ROI calculator and a savings calculator.
- **An NFC time tracker** — a Flutter app where tapping generic NFC stickers starts and stops timers for activities like studying, garybookshelf recording, editing, vibe-coding, badminton, and running, syncing to a Notion "Time Log" and eventually a Vercel-hosted report dashboard.
- **A life-design website** — an interactive, no-auth personal site meant to help you set yearly targets across financial, career, and health domains, broken into milestones, and to ingest your own daily reflections so it can rewrite your plan as your life changes. You researched frameworks like Stanford's *Designing Your Life*, and thinkers like Naval Ravikant, before scoping it.
- **An SPM English vocab-learning site**, rebranded as "garybookshelf" (Gary书柜) — daily vocab prompts, flip cards, streaks, and a themed essay store, styled with a warm "Old Paperback" palette and built on the same stack as XPLog.
- **Your own homepage/brand site** for @gary_bookshelf — styled after aliabdaal.com, combining a content hub, free resources, and a digital product shop with manual Touch 'n Go payment verification.

None of these projects sit in isolation. You consciously reuse patterns across them — the Notion-as-database approach shows up in the finance dashboard and the time tracker; the Next.js/Firebase/Vercel stack repeats across XPLog, the vocab app, and the life-design site. That's a sign you're not just building things once and moving on — you're accumulating a personal toolkit and getting faster with each project.

## The Content Creator and Marketer

Your @gary_bookshelf studygram has grown to somewhere around 45,000–47,000 followers, producing Mandarin-language, education-focused content for students aged 16–25. You think about content creation almost like a discipline with its own theory: you frame digital marketing as starting from identifying a business's actual motive — conversion, exposure, personal branding, or product promotion — and you use a three-video-type framework (about-business for conversion, about-yourself for trust, entertainment for reach) mapped to a funnel where entertainment gets the most views and about-business content gets the least.

You're honest with yourself about where your skills actually are. You rate yourself strongest at talking-head videos (which you edit using an AI tool called Kaipai) and at scripting and hook-writing. Your UGC work — especially for a brand called Thetawave — is your strongest proof point, with individual videos reaching well into the hundreds of thousands of views. You're candid about your weak spots too: editing style, thumbnails, vlogging (you're missing good BGM sources), short-story-style filming, and fast-cut transitions. You don't know paid ads, landing-page copywriting, analytics dashboards, or long-form content strategy, and you've named those as gaps you want to close deliberately rather than avoid.

You quit the Thetawave UGC work despite it being your main income source, because the work had become mechanical and stopped being creatively satisfying — a clear signal that you'll trade a proven income stream for creative ownership once you've already proven to yourself you can do the "safe" thing well. Since then you've moved into new income lanes: digital marketing for a dental clinic (with a Reel that hit 400K+ views and measurably increased bookings), and now a personal brand as a property agent under the handle "rentwithgary," partnering with CY Coliving to market rental rooms — starting with listings at Nadayu 801 near HELP University — using trilingual, trusted-advisor-toned content.

You've also started converting your existing schoolwork into a product line: you're turning your saved SPM-level Bahasa Melayu karangan essays into vocab-table study notes to sell, using a workflow where basic and advanced vocabulary are extracted from each essay, you review the selections, and the final formatted documents get filed by theme into an organized Google Drive structure.
